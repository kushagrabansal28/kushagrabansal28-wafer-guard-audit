The 0.99 F1 that wasn't

A line-monitoring model was due to ship on Friday. Its notebook reported F1 above 0.90. This is what the same model does when it is tested the way production would use it.

01

What each repair did to the score

Three bugs inflated the number. Fix them one at a time and watch it fall.

A · The claim

F1 0.990, well past the 0.90 release gate.

That is what the supplied notebook printed. It scored the model on a test set that was 50% defective and about 46% synthetic.

B · Fix the leakage

Test it on real lots, in time order.

Fit preprocessing on training rows only, split by time, drop SMOTE, and score on lots at their natural defect rate. The score falls to 0.188.

C · Fix the loss

Compare each rebuild with its own input.

The reconstruction loss compared every rebuild with every sample in the batch. With that fixed, 0.112.

D · Handle the legacy wafers

Flag the sentinel, encode the codes.

The final pipeline scores 0.105. Flagging every lot as defective scores 0.103. The model adds nothing.

02

The test set had already seen the answers

Statistics from every row, oversampling before the split, and a random split of time-ordered data.

The data

1,567 lots, in the order they were made.

Each square is one lot, read left to right and top to bottom like text. Red marks the 104 defective ones. Defects are common early and rare later: the process drifts.

What the original did

A random split.

Train, validation and test are scattered through time, so the model learns from lots that came after the ones it is tested on. Every test lot has training lots from its future.

And two more leaks

Scaling and SMOTE saw everything.

Means and standard deviations came from all 1,567 rows. SMOTE then invented 1,359 synthetic defects by blending real ones, and some of the blends landed in the test set. The result: 586 test rows, half of them defective.

The repair

Split by time.

The first 60% of lots train the model, the next 20% choose the epoch and threshold, and the last 20% are scored once. Nothing learned from a later lot can touch an earlier one.

03

A loss that never reached zero

The reconstruction was compared with every sample in the batch, not just its own input.

The loader added a channel axis, so targets had shape [B, 1, F] while reconstructions were [B, F]. PyTorch broadcast the pair to [B, B, F] without an error. The minimiser of that loss is the batch average, not a reconstruction.

04

146 lots that were not measurements

On probe station PS-L, sensor_0 is a sentinel and sensor_1 is a firmware code. The original scaled both as if they were readings.

Raw values

Two populations that share nothing.

146 lots sit at sensor_0 = 0. Their sensor_1 is not a measurement but a code: 100, 200 or 300. The other 1,421 lots form the cloud at the top right.

The original scaling

The cloud collapses.

With the sentinel and codes inflating the spread, the real wafers are squeezed into a small blob and the legacy lots land at z = −3. Codes 100 and 300 end up only 0.30 apart, a distance that means nothing.

The repair

Flag, encode, mask.

Legacy lots get an explicit flag and a one-hot firmware code, and their continuous channels are masked. Statistics come from non-legacy rows only, so the real wafers keep their full spread.

legacy lot (sentinel, firmware code)other lots

None of the 146 legacy lots is defective, against 7.3% of the rest, so this is structure a model should have been able to use. SMOTE did not invent impossible codes (0 of 1,359 rows) only because no legacy lot is a defect. A NaN in sensor_0 is a missing value, not a legacy marker. Lots missing either sensor are not drawn.

09

Would a different model do better?

One test split holds 17 defects. So the same question was asked over five consecutive future windows, every model trained only on the lots before each one.

The repaired autoencoder has not shown skill: its interval includes 0.5 and it is the weakest of the four. The simpler models have a weak but real ranking signal, so the data is not empty and the supplied architecture is a poor fit for it. The tree models' higher PR-AUC comes almost entirely from one window with 7 defects; in the others they sit near the prevalence.

Settings were fixed in advance. Only the logistic regression's regularisation is chosen, on a validation slice of the past. Nothing was tuned on these windows.

10

Do not ship it

The model as supplied has no usable skill on this data. Here is what that rests on, and how to check it yourself.

  1. The headline was an artefact.F1 0.990 came from a test set that was half synthetic and half defective, fitted on all rows and split at random.
  2. The repaired model does not beat a trivial rule.F1 0.105 against 0.103 for flagging every lot. It catches 2 of 17 defects with 19 false alarms.
